01Getting started

Start with a working station#

A practical first session: account, sound, radio control, reception and logging.

CQ Tracker combines radio operation, digital decoding, CW, station lookup, logging and propagation views. Use the installed desktop app for hardware, local files and radio control. A web page can show the interface without having access to those desktop functions.

Your first session

  1. Install the desktop version for your operating system and open it. Choose your language; this guide is available in English, Dutch, German, Spanish and French.
  2. Register your CQ Tracker account with the correct account provider. Confirm the email message, then sign in using that provider's credentials. Read Account and subscription before choosing QRZ.ru: its XML login is a separate credential.
  3. In Settings → Station, check your station callsign, locator and normal power. Use your own data; N0CALL and DEMO in this guide are examples.
  4. Select the radio's audio input and output. If you want frequency control or transmission, also configure CAT. Audio and CAT are separate paths: one working does not prove the other works.
  5. Start with reception. Check the selected band, mode and audio meter before decoding or transmitting. Once a contact is complete, inspect the callsign, time, frequency and reports in the logging form before saving.

Read the screen state

An empty log is normal on a new installation. A stopped decoder, disconnected radio or missing data feed is not evidence of an empty band. The examples in this guide show real app screens, sometimes with an isolated demonstration profile and disconnected hardware. They demonstrate controls and layout, not a measured live radio contact.

Keep the first setup simple: one radio-control application, one selected sound interface and one receiving mode. Add external logs, reporting and remote control after this basic path works. The troubleshooting guide separates connection, sound, decoding and account problems so you can test one cause at a time.

02Getting started

Accounts, trial and subscription#

Choose QRZ.com or QRZ.ru, verify email, activate your trial and manage renewal or devices.

Your provider login identifies your account; your CQ Tracker subscription determines app access. Paying for CQ Tracker does not purchase a separate QRZ.com XML-data subscription. Both supported account providers can use the same CQ Tracker subscription features, subject to the feature's own requirements.

Choose the correct login

  • QRZ.com: use your QRZ.com username and password. A QRZ.com Logbook API key belongs in log synchronization settings, not in the account password field. QRZ.com may require its own data subscription for extended XML profile data; see its official XML documentation.
  • QRZ.ru: request an XML API account through your QRZ.ru personal settings, XML API → Create account. The provider requires the appropriate callsign record in its Callbook. Use the separately issued XML username and password, preserving the username's spelling. Your ordinary website password is not a substitute. See QRZ.ru XML account help.

Changing provider clears the entered password. Do not create a second provider account to recover an existing CQ Tracker subscription: equal callsigns do not automatically merge accounts. A station callsign is profile information, not a way to change the identity linked to your paid account.

Register and activate

Follow registration through callsign confirmation, email and provider verification. Open the confirmation email, then sign in to the desktop app. An existing registration offers a login reminder instead of creating a duplicate. Check spam folders; if the message does not arrive, request a reminder rather than repeatedly registering.

The trial starts on first successful activation after email confirmation and provider login, not when the installer is downloaded. The current default is 14 days, but the server can configure it: the end date displayed in the app is authoritative. Registration does not require a payment card. Letting the trial expire does not itself create a paid subscription, and signing in again does not restart a consumed trial. Mobile-device pairing currently requires a paid subscription and is unavailable during the trial.

Payment, renewal and computers

The current in-app offer is €49 per year. Confirm the actual price and terms in Stripe Checkout before purchasing; that checkout is authoritative. Purchasing the annual subscription enables recurring annual billing. Turning automatic renewal off schedules cancellation at the end of the paid period; it does not normally end access immediately. Manage the subscription from Settings → Subscription and the Stripe account portal offered there.

A normal subscription permits up to two active computers. Your account may have a lower limit; the limit shown on the activation screen is authoritative. When it is reached, use that screen to revoke the activation of a computer you no longer use before activating the new one. Reinstalling or restoring a backup is not a new subscription. After payment, reopen Settings → Subscription and check the refreshed status. Follow any activation instructions shown by the access screen. For a provider-rate-limit message, wait a few seconds before retrying; QRZ.ru deliberately restricts request frequency.

03Getting started

Connect your radio step by step#

Separate USB audio from CAT, select the right port and confirm reception before testing PTT.

Most setups need two connections: audio carries received and transmitted sound, while CAT reads or changes frequency, mode and supported radio functions. A USB cable may expose both, but they still need separate selections in the app. Radios without USB audio need a suitable sound interface; radios without CAT can be used in audio-only mode with manual tuning.

Connect in this order

  1. Identify the exact radio and interface model. Check the manufacturer's driver instructions first; some Windows drivers must be installed before connecting the USB cable.
  2. Connect the radio with a data-capable USB cable and turn it on. Confirm that the operating system lists the serial port and, where supported, the radio sound device.
  3. Close other programs using that CAT port. In Settings → Radio, use Scan ports or Autodetect. Treat autodetection as a proposal: verify the radio model, interface, port and audio choices.
  4. Choose the control method. Serial CAT uses the radio's serial protocol. Hamlib uses the selected model profile. FLrig connects to a running FLrig service; Network connects to a configured control server such as rigctld. Audio only provides no CAT tuning.
  5. Match the radio's baud rate and other serial parameters. Use the actual port shown on this computer, not the example port number from another operator.
  6. Select Connect. This saves the current radio configuration before connecting. Check that the app reads the real frequency and mode. Turn the radio dial slightly: the displayed frequency should follow. Use Test CAT for a control check before considering a PTT test.
  7. Select the radio's audio input in Settings → Audio, then confirm received sound and a moving level indicator in the chosen operating page.

Before a transmit test

Test PTT can key the real transmitter. Prepare the radio, antenna or suitable test load and power level accordingly; follow your equipment instructions. Confirm the selected PTT method and audio output, then verify that the radio returns to receive when the test ends. A successful CAT read does not prove correct PTT wiring or transmit audio.

For a Yaesu FTDX101D on Windows, the manufacturer's instructions distinguish Enhanced COM for CAT from Standard COM for PTT/CW functions. Their COM numbers vary by computer. Do not copy those choices to an unrelated radio. See Drivers for the manufacturer sources and Radio settings for individual fields.

04Getting started

Drivers and required software#

Find manufacturer drivers and distinguish a missing serial device from missing radio-control software.

Settings → Dependencies shows the tools detected by CQ Tracker and provides links for radio drivers. A USB serial driver makes the radio port available to the operating system. Hamlib or FLrig provides radio-control software. Installing one does not replace the other, and a radio audio device can appear even when CAT is not yet configured.

Choose a source for your hardware

  • Yaesu FTDX101D: use the official model downloads and its USB installation manual. For the documented Windows setup, install the driver before connecting USB. Enhanced COM serves CAT; Standard COM serves the documented PTT/CW functions. USB audio is a separate device.
  • Icom: select your exact model on Icom's support site. The documented driver package for the IC-7300/IC-7610 group is not a universal package for every Icom radio.
  • Kenwood: consult the virtual COM-port instructions and model software downloads. Follow the specified installation order.
  • Interface chips: use Silicon Labs CP210x or FTDI VCP drivers only when that chip is actually present.

On macOS and Linux, use the operating system's supported driver or the matching manufacturer package. Do not disable operating-system security to install an unrelated driver. Reconnect or restart only as the manufacturer's instructions require, then scan the ports again.

Check what is still missing

If no serial port appears in the operating system, check the cable, USB hub, radio power and driver first. If a port appears but CAT cannot connect, check port ownership and the radio parameters. If Hamlib is selected, review its dependency status and the official Hamlib downloads. A network connection requires its server to be running; the default local rigctld endpoint is commonly 127.0.0.1:4532, and the app's FLrig default is http://127.0.0.1:12345. Match your server configuration rather than opening CAT ports to the public internet.

05Getting started

Operate the five Shack views#

Use the operating console, live CQ monitor, sked planner, QSO map and solar view together.

The Shack groups five related views behind its top tabs. Use the explanation-tooltip switch if you want a reminder of each view's purpose. The separate Dashboard button opens a customizable layout; it is not another radio connection.

Operating console

Use the console for frequency/mode status, received activity, station details and logging. Confirm the connected radio before using tuning or PTT controls. A callsign detail panel lets you inspect the station before working or logging it. A spot, detection or QRZ profile is supporting information; review the actual exchange yourself.

Live CQ monitor

The CQ view follows current calls and QSO activity. Select a callsign to inspect available details, use the configured CQ template or recording for your own calling workflow, and stop an automatic calling sequence when a station replies. Filtering by band or mode makes the display easier to interpret. Public activity can be delayed, and a highlighted call does not mean the local receiver has decoded it.

Sked planner

Use skeds to arrange contacts and inspect accepted or open requests. Check the time shown, station, band, mode and notes before accepting. Use the available cancel/withdraw actions when plans change so the other participant is not left with a stale arrangement. A reminder helps you prepare; it does not tune, transmit or complete a contact by itself.

Map and solar views

The map connects station locations and activity to geography; missing coordinates can leave a real contact without a plotted point. The solar view provides indices, observations and propagation context. Use them to choose what to listen for, then verify conditions at your station. If a view displays an access or data-availability message, resolve that state instead of treating a blank panel as an all-clear report.

06Getting started

Build your personal Dashboard#

Arrange useful widgets, resize the mosaic and keep a stable operating layout.

The Dashboard gathers selected widgets into a tiled workspace. It is useful for keeping a log, clock, map and propagation information visible together. Start with a few large widgets; a dense arrangement can make small controls difficult to read on a laptop.

Assemble a layout

  1. Open Dashboard and unlock the layout if the editing palette is hidden.
  2. Choose a scaffold from the layout options or start with the current arrangement.
  3. Select widgets in the palette. Active choices indicate which widgets are already present; selecting one again can remove it.
  4. Drag tile dividers to adjust space. Move a tile using its title bar and the mosaic's drop zones.
  5. Lock the layout when finished so routine operating clicks do not rearrange it.

Scaffolds reorganize the available tiles; inspect each widget afterward, particularly text-heavy logs and maps. Clear-all removes the layout's widgets. It is a workspace action, not a backup tool or a way to delete the underlying QSO database. If you clear the layout accidentally, add the wanted widgets from the palette again.

Pages, pins and separate windows

A page's pin/integration action can place another app view into your workspace. Some pages also provide Detach, which moves that view into a separate desktop window. Use these actions deliberately: the page may display a placeholder while it is shown elsewhere. See Workspace, radar and ticker for returning or unpinning views.

Widgets display the data their source makes available. A propagation widget requires its data source, and radio controls require the real connection. A locked layout freezes editing, not incoming data or the radio's state. If a tile looks empty, check the underlying source/page and its filters before rebuilding the entire dashboard.

07Getting started

Workspace, radar, ticker and floating views#

Navigate confidently, inspect detected callsigns and recover pinned or detached pages.

Use the main sidebar to change tasks. The settings menu belongs to the Settings page; the Shack tabs belong to the Shack. Keeping these navigation levels separate helps you find the current controls, especially when a view is embedded in another page or moved to its own window.

Radar and the ticker

Radar brings detected callsigns and station details into focus. Select a row or search/inspect the intended callsign, then check the source, frequency and age of the detection. A matching QRZ entry can help identify a station but does not prove that speech recognition heard every character correctly.

The floating ticker keeps incoming activity visible outside the main workspace. Selecting a callsign can invoke the station/tuning workflow; verify the resulting radio frequency and mode. If the radio cannot tune, inspect the station information and connection message instead of repeatedly clicking the same item. Feed activity, speech detections and locally decoded signals are different sources.

Pin and detach

The pin action keeps a page available within the app's integrated workspace. Use the corresponding unpin/return action to restore the ordinary page. Detach opens a supported page or panel in a separate desktop window, useful on a second monitor. The original page may show where that view is now displayed. Bring it back using the provided return action rather than assuming its controls have vanished.

When a window is narrow, toolbars can wrap and parts of a page scroll. Scroll the content area to reach bottom actions. Move a detached window back onto the visible monitor before unplugging a display where possible. If an embedded page is cramped, resize its tile or open it in its ordinary view.

Appearance/layout changes do not establish radio connections, enable a subscription or save a QSO. After moving a control panel, confirm its current RX/TX state before using it. The separate Signal Scope page is a useful candidate to pin beside the operating console.

08Getting started

Troubleshoot one layer at a time#

A symptom-based path for login, devices, silent audio, failed decodes, transmission and saved data.

Start by noting the page, exact error, selected mode and whether the problem began after changing a device or setting. Keep a working configuration available. Change one thing and repeat the same test; changing the radio, audio and decoder settings together hides the cause.

Cannot enter the app

Check the chosen provider and credential type. QRZ.ru needs its issued XML login; QRZ.com log synchronization keys do not replace the account password. Confirm your email, inspect the subscription end date and resolve any computer limit. A website session is not a hardware-capable desktop session. Wait before retrying a provider rate limit. Account reminders go to the registered email address; they do not move the subscription to another identity.

Radio is disconnected or frequency is wrong

Check power and cable, then whether the operating system lists the CAT port. Close any competing application. Rescan ports after reconnecting USB, confirm model and baud rate, then select Connect to save the configuration and connect. For network or FLrig control, confirm the local service is running and its host/port matches. Do not test transmission to diagnose a read-frequency problem.

Silence or a flat meter

Choose radio → computer as input, not the laptop microphone by accident. Check radio USB/data audio routing, receive level, mute controls and operating-system input permission. Desktop apps may need microphone permission even for a line or USB sound input: see Windows guidance or macOS microphone access. Re-select the device after unplugging it. For audible monitoring, select speakers or headphones rather than routing receive audio back into the transmitter.

Sound is present but decoding is poor

Confirm the actual signal mode and band, then inspect input level. Reduce overload before increasing sensitivity or decoder depth. Digital modes need the correct slot/submode and a correctly synchronized system clock. CW needs a stable tone and clean keying; adjust tone/auto-tune, gain and sensitivity while listening to the same signal. Network spots are not proof that the same signal reaches your antenna. No software setting can guarantee decoding through interference or incompatible modulation.

TX is disabled, silent or remains active

Look for a missing CAT connection, blank message, invalid CW character, monitor-only mode, selected output device or an active transmit operation. Confirm the radio's data-input/PTT configuration separately from receive audio. Use the page's Stop/Halt control to end the operation and verify RX on the physical radio. If it does not release, stop the transmitter using its own controls and inspect the interface configuration before another test.

Missing contacts or pictures

Check date, band and mode filters before assuming records were lost. Clearing a current digital activity list is different from deleting saved log data; inspect Settings → QSO transactions. A spot or decoded message is not automatically a completed QSO. For failed synchronization, check the saved local contact first, then the destination's test/status. For maps or external data, distinguish a connection error from an empty result. When asking for support, include the app version, OS, radio/interface model and exact steps, with passwords, API keys and pairing links removed.

09Settings

Find and save your settings#

A map of every normal settings section and how its save controls behave.

Open Settings from the sidebar. Select a section in the settings menu; on a small window, scroll the menu or content to reach the remaining controls. The top Save action applies the main settings form and displays success or an error. Some panels, such as synchronization, backups and configuration profiles, have their own action buttons. Read that panel's result rather than assuming every change saves automatically.

Where each task belongs

| Section | Use it for | | --- | --- | | Audio; Radio; Dependencies | Sound devices, CAT/PTT, connection checks and required drivers/tools | | Subscription | Trial, paid plan, renewal and computer activation | | Frequencies | Saved band/frequency/mode rows and tuning shortcuts | | Station; QRZ | Station information and provider account credentials | | CSID; CQ templates; CQ audio | Identification settings, calling sequences and recorded voice calls | | Logging; QSO transactions | ADIF preferences, duplicate window and completed digital exchanges | | Log synchronization; QSL email | External log destinations and the sender used for emailed QSL cards | | Reporting; Feeds; Callsign listener; Remote | Public reports, incoming activity, speech detection and mobile control | | Backup; Configuration profiles | Complete data backup versus reusable configuration snapshots | | Language | Interface language and Shack-tab explanation tooltips |

Frequencies

Add a row with the intended band, MHz value, mode and optional label. Edit or remove an obsolete row using its actions. Check the units: entering hertz into an MHz field gives the wrong result. Tune needs connected CAT. Reset replaces the list with the app's defaults, so preserve custom entries first. A saved frequency is an operating shortcut, not a statement that transmission is permitted everywhere.

Language and explanations

Choose English, Dutch, German, Spanish or French. Labels move with the language, but account names, radio models and logged callsigns do not need translating. If a section shows a desktop-only notice or unavailable device list, open the installed app instead of treating the browser preview as a configuration test. Advanced development/admin sections are not required for ordinary station operation.

10Settings

Audio input, output and levels#

Route receive and transmit audio correctly and recover after a device changes.

Input is sound arriving at CQ Tracker, normally from your radio's receiver. Output is sound sent by CQ Tracker, normally to the radio's transmit audio input. A listening monitor is a separate choice. Selecting a similarly named speaker or microphone can produce plausible activity while monitoring the wrong source.

Set up the sound path

  1. Connect and power the audio interface before opening the device list.
  2. Choose the radio's receive device as input. Start a receiver or decoder and verify that tuning to a signal changes the meter or waterfall.
  3. Choose the radio's transmit device as output. Use a controlled test only after checking the radio's input routing and transmit state.
  4. Select a sample rate supported by the hardware. The app offers 48,000, 44,100, 24,000, 16,000 and 8,000 Hz; 48,000 Hz is a practical starting choice for a compatible USB radio interface.
  5. Save and inspect the result. Match radio audio can apply detected suggestions; confirm them, especially when multiple USB interfaces have similar names.

System default follows the operating system's current default. That is convenient for a simple setup but can change when a headset, display or Bluetooth device is connected. An explicit radio device is usually easier to diagnose. After a cable reconnect or driver change, stop audio, re-select the device and start again.

Receive level and monitoring

Aim for clear activity with room for stronger signals; a meter pinned at its top suggests overload. Adjust the radio/interface receive level before compensating with large software gain. Grant the app input permission in the operating system even when the source is a line input. Avoid speech enhancement or noise suppression in the radio sound path when it distorts digital tones.

CW has its own input chooser and receiver gain/sensitivity controls. Its Monitor switch plays received audio; the monitor-level knob controls the local CW sidetone. CQ audio has a separate optional listening output. These controls are not calibrated RF power controls. Use headphones or a suitable monitor path to prevent feedback, and keep operating-system sounds away from the radio transmit output.

11Settings

Radio settings explained#

Choose a control provider, serial parameters, PTT method and connection safeguards.

Configure Settings → Radio for the actual interface connected to your station. Use Connect your radio for the initial sequence; this chapter explains the choices you may need to adjust afterward.

Connection provider

Audio only leaves frequency and mode under manual radio control. Serial CAT needs the correct serial port and protocol. Hamlib adds a model list; filter by the exact model and review the profile defaults applied by that selection. Network requires a reachable control server and matching host/port. FLrig uses its running local service. A stored address alone does not start another program. The app can offer available alternatives when a dependency is missing; verify the provider shown after making changes.

For a serial connection, match baud rate, data bits, stop bits, parity and handshake to the radio menu/manual. Set the Icom CI-V address where that protocol requires it. Forced RTS/DTR and a separate PTT port are hardware-specific; incorrect line settings can unexpectedly key some interfaces. Do not enable them simply because connection failed.

Tune and transmit behavior

Choose the supported PTT method for the interface. Target mode controls whether tuning leaves the mode unchanged or requests USB/data operation. Split settings affect receive/transmit frequency behavior where supported; confirm the result on the physical radio before operating split. Automatic connection is useful once a saved configuration works reliably.

The PTT watchdog limits a continuous keying interval. The option to block TX when CAT is disconnected prevents a transmit action when the app cannot establish the expected control state. Keep these safeguards consistent with your operating setup; they do not replace checking the radio itself.

Use Connect to save the current radio configuration and then connect. Verify frequency readback, then use Test CAT. Test PTT is a separate action that can transmit. Disconnect before changing cables or assigning the same serial port to another program.

12Settings

Station identity, profiles and QRZ#

Keep your operator information accurate and use the correct provider credentials.

Your station callsign, locator and default power populate operating and logging views. Your provider username identifies the account used to sign in. They are related pieces of information, but editing a local station field does not transfer a subscription or change a provider identity.

Station information

Enter a valid Maidenhead locator for your operating position and verify it after moving. It affects maps, distances, reporting and satellite calculations. Default power is a logging default; it does not set the transmitter's RF output. If the callsign field is locked, do not create a second registration to work around it. Use the existing account's login/reminder flow and resolve an incorrect identity with support.

Station profiles describe different equipment or locations. Add a descriptive name such as Home or Portable, fill in transceiver, power, antenna and locator, then save. Select an existing profile to inspect or edit it and mark the appropriate default. Review the equipment details used for your next contact after switching profiles. Deleting a station profile is different from deleting the logged contacts made with that station.

QRZ account settings

The account section identifies QRZ.com or QRZ.ru. To update credentials, enter the username and a new valid password for that provider and save. Existing passwords are not displayed. Use the login provider selector when intentionally changing providers; matching callsigns do not combine accounts.

QRZ.ru requires its separately issued XML account, not its normal website password. QRZ.com profile access uses the account login, while QRZ.com logbook synchronization uses a separate Logbook API key in Log synchronization. Keep these credentials in their matching fields. A successful sign-in with limited profile data can indicate the external provider's data entitlement rather than a missing CQ Tracker subscription. See Accounts and subscription for registration and recovery.

13Settings

CQ templates, voice recordings and CSID#

Prepare calling messages and recordings, and understand the controls that can start transmission.

Prepare transmit material before a session. A template describes a call, a recording contains actual audio, and CSID adds a supported identification signal. These settings serve different purposes; enabling one does not prove the radio's PTT or audio path is connected.

CQ templates and repeated calls

Choose an existing template or edit the available templates for the message you intend to send. Check the expanded station information before calling. Automatic-call options control whether calls repeat, the maximum count and the interval. The panel-visibility option controls access to those repeat controls, not the radio connection. The spacebar-PTT preference changes keyboard operation; verify focus and behavior before typing while the station is ready to transmit. Stop the calling sequence before changing the radio or leaving the operating position.

CQ audio

Use Record new to create a voice call, give it a recognizable name and inspect its duration. Test playback before using it in a live call. Delete obsolete recordings carefully so you do not remove the one referenced by your operating routine. The optional monitor output is for hearing playback locally; choose headphones or speakers, not a second path back into the radio. If you cannot hear a test, check that monitoring choice separately from the transmit output in Audio settings.

CSID and the call trigger

The enable switch controls CSID transmission. Select the tone profile appropriate to your setup, set amplitude conservatively and review the band blacklist. Amplitude is an audio setting, not RF watts. The CQ trigger can use a button or microphone VOX. With microphone triggering selected, the VOX threshold determines which audio activity starts the trigger; a noisy microphone or overly sensitive threshold can trigger unexpectedly. Verify behavior while supervising the radio. These controls do not replace the mode-specific transmit controls on the CW or Digimode page.

14Settings

Logging, transaction history and external logs#

Set ADIF preferences, inspect digital exchanges, synchronize destinations and configure QSL email.

The local QSO log, completed digital transaction history and external services are separate records. First confirm that a contact is saved locally. Then inspect synchronization or email delivery; a failed external upload does not mean the local contact disappeared.

Logging and transaction archive

Choose an ADIF path that exists and is writable. Review the duplicate window in minutes for your operating style; a repeat warning needs inspection, not an automatic assumption that the QSO is invalid. The QSO transactions section stores completed digital exchanges independently of the current activity list.

Use the search and filters together, then read the shown/total count before exporting. Clear a restrictive filter when a known exchange appears missing. Clearing the Digimode screen is not the same as removing this archive.

Log synchronization

The panel supports QRZ.com Logbook, N1MM, HRD, Log4OM, Wavelog, file drop, LoTW, Club Log, eQSL and HRDLOG.net. Use the credential type requested by each destination. QRZ.com needs its Logbook API key; Wavelog needs its URL, key and station identifier; desktop UDP destinations need matching host/port settings in the receiving program. File drop needs a writable path. Do not route the same QSO through several programs that send it back to each other.

LoTW upload uses the configured TQSL installation and station location; downloading confirmations uses the separate website login fields. Club Log, eQSL and HRDLOG.net have their own account/upload settings. Use individual tests or the test-all action after saving. A connection test is not proof that an award confirmation has been granted; inspect the destination's result and the actual uploaded contact.

QSL email

The guided Gmail setup uses an app password where Google makes that option available. For another provider, enter its SMTP host, port, security mode, username and permitted credentials. The interface offers STARTTLS and TLS options; follow the mail provider's current requirements rather than assuming all providers accept the same password format. Set the From address/name accurately. The test-recipient option can send real test messages, including a PNG attachment; use a mailbox you control. Review the result before enabling any automated QSL sending.

15Settings

Reporting, activity feeds, speech and mobile#

Distinguish incoming information from public reports and remote operating access.

Integrations have different effects. Feeds bring information into the app; public reporting sends station/reception information out; speech recognition derives callsigns from audio; remote control permits another device to operate the desktop station. Enable each only for the task you want.

Public reporting

Reporting is initially off. A valid account session and usable locator are required. Set the optional antenna description, save and inspect any blocked-locator notice. The test action submits to a public service, so use accurate station details. Automatically extracted detections can be tentative: verify a callsign before treating it as a completed contact.

Incoming feeds

Available sources include Club Log live streams, HRDLOG recent activity, HamQTH RBN and a HamQTH logbook watchlist. The watchlist needs its requested account credentials and comma-separated callsigns. A DX-cluster connection needs the selected host, port and login callsign; while connected, its spots can replace the public cluster feed used by the band map. A feed shows what that service reports, not necessarily what your receiver hears. Check source and timestamp when comparing entries.

Callsign listener

The listener offers an enable switch, input source/device, microphone gain, language, minimum confidence, voice-activity threshold and processing threads. Start with one clear source. Raising confidence reduces doubtful results but can miss weak calls; a low voice threshold can admit noise. Automatic QRZ opening and digital-exchange options add actions to detection, so enable them after basic recognition is useful. Use the microphone test to check levels and permissions. A guessed callsign should be checked against the actual exchange before logging.

Mobile control

The desktop must remain running and connected to the station. Enable remote control, wait for the agent status and generate the phone or tablet pairing code. Codes expire; generate a new one if needed. Inspect paired devices and disconnect one you no longer use. Pairing requires a paid subscription in the current app and is not available during the trial. Losing the phone's connection does not replace checking the desktop and radio's operating state. Disable remote control when the session is finished.

16Settings

Backups and configuration profiles#

Protect complete app data and distinguish a restore from applying a reusable setup.

Use Backup for recovering the application's stored data. Use Configuration profiles for named, reusable groups of settings. They are not interchangeable: a configuration profile is not a copy of your logbook, and restoring a full backup can replace the current data on this computer.

Export and restore

  1. Open the desktop app and choose Settings → Backup.
  2. Select Export backup, wait for success and locate the resulting file. Keep a separate copy on storage you trust; do not depend solely on the same disk as the running app.
  3. Before restoring, export the current data too, especially if this computer contains newer QSOs.
  4. Choose the existing backup file. Review the confirmation: restoring overwrites current application data and restarts the app.
  5. After restart, inspect the log, settings and operating station. Recheck account activation, credentials and local device paths as needed; a backup does not add another licensed computer or guarantee that another machine uses the same serial/audio names.

Keep backups private: logs and settings can contain personal information. An export or successful file copy does not by itself mean the file is encrypted. Store recovery copies with suitable access restrictions and verify that you can locate them before you need them.

Reusable configurations

Save a descriptive profile such as Home, Portable or Contest. The profile captures the allowed non-sensitive configuration, such as QTH, feed choices, map filters, dashboard layout and interface preferences; it is not intended to export passwords, account data or licenses. Export/import transfers that profile file. Apply only after reviewing the replacement/reload confirmation, then check the resulting station context. Delete removes a saved profile, not a complete application backup.

The Field Day preset prepares the app's corresponding operating configuration and portable pack. Check the selected contest, station details and actual connectivity before operating: applying a preset is not proof that your radio or every external service works offline.

17Working callsigns

Log a contact accurately#

Review a station, complete the QSO form and distinguish a saved contact from a live detection.

Log a QSO after an actual contact has taken place. Incoming spots, decoded CQ messages and station lookups help prepare a contact but are not themselves confirmations that you worked the station.

Save a contact

  1. Open the station detail or Log action from your operating view. You can also use the logging control on the CW page after entering the other station's callsign.
  2. Verify every callsign character. Correct a detection or lookup result before it reaches the log.
  3. Check band, frequency and mode against the radio, particularly if you tuned since opening the form. Review the displayed date/time rather than assuming the computer clock is correct.
  4. Enter or confirm the reports sent and received, power and relevant notes. Use any available station-profile selection to describe the equipment actually used.
  5. Save, wait for confirmation and check the local log entry. Review a duplicate warning in context: the same station may legitimately be worked again on another band or at another time.

What happens afterward

Enabled synchronization destinations can receive the saved contact. If an upload fails, inspect its result without creating a second local QSO as a workaround. A digital exchange can also appear in the separate QSO transactions archive; this keeps its completed-exchange history even when the current activity list is cleared.

Use log filters carefully when finding a saved entry. Correct an existing record rather than adding another copy solely to repair a typo. Export the required ADIF/other supported records for interchange and retain a full backup before large imports or restores. A QSL card uses contact data from the log; inspect it before sending to avoid propagating an incorrect callsign or report.

Logging settings explains the ADIF path, duplicate window, transaction export, external logs and email setup.

18Working callsigns

Read and use the Band Map#

Filter band activity, inspect station entries and tune with awareness of the data source.

The Band Map arranges station activity by frequency. Use it to find an occupied segment or an interesting callsign, then listen before calling. It can combine information from external sources and local activity; a spot is not a measurement of reception at your antenna.

Find a station

  1. Select the band and the mode/source filters relevant to your operation.
  2. Look at the frequency scale and station labels. Zoom or resize the view when entries overlap.
  3. Select a callsign to open its detail/entry panel. Check the station identity, frequency, mode and timestamp.
  4. If CAT is connected, use the tuning action and confirm the physical radio follows. Without CAT, tune the radio manually.
  5. Work the station, then log the actual contact through the logging workflow.

The radio/VFO marker shows the tuned position when a connection provides it. It can help explain why the displayed activity and what you hear differ. A selected spot does not automatically adjust every radio-specific filter or data-input setting.

Keep the source in mind

External feed settings determine which activity reaches the map. A connected DX-cluster source can replace the public HamQTH cluster feed. Delayed or repeated spots are possible, and an old entry may remain visible after a station has moved. Narrow filters, a disconnected feed or missing location information can make the display sparse without indicating a software fault.

The Signal Scope displays the connected station's spectrum/waterfall path, which is a different view from a network list of band activity. Use both when you need to compare reported activity with the signal actually reaching your receiver. Check Feeds and reporting if expected sources are absent.

19Working callsigns

Digimode: receive, reply and control the display#

Work with mode and slot settings, decode lists, transmit sequences, waterfall controls and top-bar tools.

Digimode combines the decoder, radio status, frequency controls, decoded messages and transmit sequence. Begin in Monitor only with transmission disabled. Select a mode and band that match the received signal, then start the engine and inspect the connection and audio state.

Receive and interpret the display

Select the mode, band, decode depth and any mode-specific submode/slot duration. The radio status must agree with the physical transceiver. A deeper decode search can take more processing; it is not a substitute for the correct mode, clock and audio level. Allow a complete receive interval before judging the result. Review SNR, timing offset and the decoded message, and distinguish received decodes from external spots.

RX and TX frequency fields in the DX controls are audio offsets in Hz, not the radio's dial frequency. TX = RX and RX = TX copy one offset to the other. Lock keeps them linked; split lets the transmit path differ as configured. Selecting a target callsign fills the target area; clearing it removes that target, not saved QSO history. A decode filter can hide activity outside the chosen context, so check it when rows seem missing.

Calling and replying

  1. Verify your callsign/grid and a suitable TX offset. Inspect the message that will be sent and whether the next slot is RX or TX.
  2. Select a decoded CQ or target and review the reply sequence. A queued message waits for its scheduled opportunity; it is not necessarily already on air.
  3. Enable TX only when ready. Use Call CQ for your own calling sequence; watch the current/queued indication and the slot progress.
  4. Inspect reports and acknowledgements as the QSO advances. Save/log the completed contact and review the result.
  5. Use Halt TX to interrupt transmission control, Disable TX to remove transmit permission, or the main Stop action to stop the running engine. Verify receive state on the radio.

Auto-Seq advances the exchange; Auto TX, Call first, One QSO, Skip TX1/grid and the autoresponder affect which actions happen automatically. Start with a simple supervised sequence before combining them. Directed CQ, maximum unanswered attempts and multiple-answer targets change who is called and how long the sequence continues. Tune/test-tone actions can produce real output: prepare the radio before using them.

Waterfall and scope

The waterfall shows signal history across the selected audio span; the spectrum shows the current distribution. The RX/TX markers correspond to the chosen offsets. Start frequency and span alter the visible range. Display gain and waterfall speed change readability, not the radio's RF output. Zoom-on-frequency follows the selected area. Decode ticker, oscilloscope and 3D options expose additional views; resize them to keep controls readable. Hide/pop-out affects the display location, and Show waterfall brings a hidden view back.

Use the palette popover for colors, scope sensitivity for the trace scale and marker/TX-bar switches for annotations. A displayed waveform or outgoing-symbol visualization is not a calibrated RF power measurement. Read the source/status indication: a stopped, preview or no-audio display does not prove live reception.

Top-bar tools

| Tool | Purpose and what to check | | --- | --- | | Propagation; Awards | Inspect band context or logged-contact progress without replacing the current radio mode | | Settings | Review station, sound devices, TX delay, frequency presets, decoder options, display filters, colors, macros and operating automation | | Fox/Hound; Beacon | Mode-specific controls, shown for the relevant mode; use them only for a matching operating procedure | | Bridge | Configure/inspect the supported external-program link; avoid running competing transmit controllers | | Time sync | Inspect clock/timing information; synchronize the computer before troubleshooting slot-based decoding | | Multi-answer | Review reply targets and automation choices before allowing multiple responses | | Personalize | Adjust theme, palette and type size; appearance changes do not improve the underlying decode | | Pin; Detach | Move the view into a workspace or separate desktop window |

The older DigiWave route, if exposed in your installation, is a separate legacy view. Use this current Digimode guide for the main operating console; do not run two independent transmit workflows against the same radio.

20Working callsigns

CW radio: one RX/TX conversation#

Receive Morse, send supervised messages, adjust the radio knobs and save the exchange.

The CW page presents a radio-style console with one chronological conversation. RX and TX have distinct markers, colors and alignment, so you can follow an exchange without switching between separate text windows. Short receive pauses stay in the ongoing passage; a longer silence or an intervening transmission can start another bubble.

Receive first

Choose the actual audio input and press RX Start listening. Allow input permission when requested. Tune to a clear CW tone and check the RX level/tone strip. Auto-tune follows a detected tone; if several signals compete, use a stable selected tone and a narrower/cleaner radio passband. Adjust Gain for level and Sensitivity for detection. Excess gain can turn noise into false keying rather than improve reception.

The Monitor switch lets you hear received audio. Check decoded words against what you hear: fading, interference, uneven keying or a wrong tone can cause errors. WPM/SNR/lock status are diagnostic indicators, not guarantees of a correct callsign. Stop listening when changing input devices or finishing the receive session.

Send and tune

A supported connected CAT radio is required for the page's Send action. Enter a short message or insert a preset, inspect its text and resolve any unsupported-character warning. Set Speed in WPM, Tone in Hz and the local monitor level. The VFO knob and step buttons tune a connected radio; the CW button requests CW mode when available. Frequency memories are shortcuts, so verify the physical radio and the band before sending.

Drag a knob or focus it and use the arrow keys for controlled adjustments. Tone adjustment can preview the local sidetone. Press Send CW only when ready, watch the active text and use Stop to interrupt it. A stopped/failed bubble remains identified as such; do not assume the pending remainder was transmitted. A local sidetone is not proof that RF reached the antenna.

Understand the meters and save controls

The meters follow the app's live audio paths: RX reflects received audio and TX follows the local sidetone after its gain control. They are audio indicators in dBFS, not calibrated radio S-meter, SWR or RF watt readings. Lowering the local monitor level can reduce the TX meter while CAT still controls the radio. A quiet meter therefore needs a signal-path check before drawing a conclusion about RF output.

Save conversation saves the exchange; Save RX saves received text only. Clear RX removes RX text from this view while retaining TX. New session clears both directions and starts a fresh conversation: save first if you want to keep it. If you scrolled back, use the follow action to return to new messages. Enter the other station's callsign and open the logging form to record the actual QSO separately from the transcript.

21Maps & data

QSO Map: contacts and activity in context#

Use geographical views and filters without confusing missing locations with missing contacts.

The QSO Map places station information and activity on a geographical view. Use it to understand where a contact is located and how paths relate to your station. Check your locator first: an incorrect home position affects distances, bearings and the map center.

Explore deliberately

  1. Choose the map/source view for the information you want to inspect, such as your contacts or current activity.
  2. Set the band, mode and time filters. Start broadly if looking for an older contact.
  3. Zoom or move the map, then select a station or path to inspect the available details.
  4. Compare the callsign and time with the log or activity source before using an operating action.
  5. Reset or widen filters if the map becomes empty unexpectedly.

A contact needs usable geographical information to be plotted. Missing grid/coordinates, a failed lookup or a restrictive time filter can leave a valid log entry absent from the map. Conversely, a network activity marker can appear without a completed contact in your log. Use the log as the record of what you worked.

Map projections and display layers help readability; they do not change the stored contact. At world scale, nearby stations can overlap. Zoom in or inspect the list/details instead of assuming a single visible marker represents every QSO.

For frequency-ordered activity, use Band Map. These views complement one another but do not measure whether propagation currently supports a specific contact. Listen and verify the actual radio path.

22Maps & data

Plan with the DX Calendar#

Filter expeditions and events, inspect details and manage reminders.

The DX Calendar helps you plan around announced activity. Schedules describe expected operation; conditions, travel or organizer updates can change them. Check the event's source/details before relying on a particular band or time.

Find relevant events

Use the event-type and time-period controls to start with upcoming or current activity. Narrow by band/mode, search text or an explicit date range. Switch between the available list/calendar presentations to compare events. Refresh when you need newly retrieved information and read the resulting status instead of assuming an old list updated successfully.

Open an event to inspect its callsign, dates and available operating information. Check whether times are displayed as UTC or local time, and compare them with your operating schedule. Alerts and worked state

Keep the event page/source available for final schedule checks, then use the operating views to tune, listen, work and log the contact. Signal Scope and the connected radio#

Read the local spectrum/waterfall path and understand the disconnected screen.

Signal Scope is a separate page for inspecting the connected station's spectrum/waterfall view. It requires an active CAT radio connection. Until that connection exists, the page shows a clear connection message and a button leading to Radio settings; it does not fill the screen with invented live signals.

Establish the source

  1. Connect the radio in Settings → Radio and verify actual frequency/mode readback.
  2. Configure receive audio for that radio and confirm a real input signal.
  3. Return to Signal Scope. Check the displayed dial frequency and band against the transceiver.
  4. Inspect the spectrum and waterfall as you tune across a known received signal. Use the available view controls to adjust its readability.

The frequency labels combine the dial context with the displayed signal span. Ordinary CAT does not automatically provide a full hardware bandscope or broadband IQ stream: the available source determines what portion of the signal you see. Network spot overlays identify reported activity and are distinct from the measured audio spectrum.

Diagnose a flat or misleading display

If disconnected, troubleshoot CAT first. If connected but flat, inspect the input device, radio audio routing and level. An audio spectrum can show noise or a strong tone without producing a valid decoded message; the decoder still needs a matching mode and suitable signal. Display gain, zoom and color adjustments improve visibility but do not increase RF reception sensitivity.

Pin this page beside the Band Map when you want to compare reported activity with the local receiving path. Check timestamps and sources during that comparison. Create, receive and send QSL cards#

Use the inbox, templates, sending, automation and labels with verified QSO details.

QSL Cards groups card reception and preparation into its own page. Start from an accurate saved QSO and the correct station information; a well-designed card with the wrong callsign or time is still an incorrect confirmation.

Inbox and provider connection

Use Inbox to inspect received cards and their contact information. Track awards and worked regions#

Understand the award wall, region views, parks and progress based on your saved log.

Awards summarizes progress calculated from the contacts available to the app. Use it to choose a next target and inspect which records contributed to a result. App progress is an operating aid; it is not automatic acceptance of an official award application by another organization.

Read the categories

The Wall presents earned/progress items. Continents and Countries show geographical coverage. Subdivisions gives more detailed regional targets, while Parks focuses on the supported park context. Ranks presents the app's progress/ranking view. Select a region or item to inspect its details rather than relying only on a colored badge.

Filters and award configuration affect what counts in a view. Make sure the intended band/mode and log records are included. A callsign can be correctly logged while lacking the country, subdivision or park metadata required for a particular calculation. Correct the underlying record or obtain the missing information before treating that as a lost QSO.

Update the underlying log

The QRZ.com import action requires the separately configured QRZ.com Logbook integration and its permissions. It does not import a QRZ.ru account's logbook simply because you use QRZ.ru to sign in. The award strip and Awards button in Digimode lead to the same progress context. A newly decoded station is not yet a worked region; complete and save the QSO first. External confirmations may arrive later, and an organization's final eligibility rules remain separate from the app's local progress display.

26Satellites

Satellites: tracks, sky view and imagery#

Set the observer location, update orbital data and inspect passes, favorites and alerts.

Satellite tracking combines an orbital catalog with your station position and time. Set a correct station locator before judging visibility. The app's calculated position and pass information do not by themselves confirm that a satellite's radio payload is currently operating.

Select and follow

  1. Open Satellites in the desktop app and inspect the observer location shown near the title.
  2. Use Refresh TLE when the orbital data needs updating. Wait for the result and check the displayed update time.
  3. Search the catalog, mark useful satellites as favorites and optionally show only favorites.
  4. Select a satellite's information action for details; use its map/tracking action to include it in the view.
  5. Read elevation, azimuth and pass/visibility information for your location. A below-horizon item can remain in the catalog even when it is not currently visible.

The visible-now list depends on observer position and current orbital calculation. An empty list can be correct. If the page asks for a locator or reports missing track data, fix that state before interpreting the result.

Frequencies and Doppler

During a pass, relative motion changes the received frequency: this is Doppler shift. AMSAT's operating guidance explains frequency correction.

The details show catalog RX/downlink and TX/uplink frequencies, plus position and speed. Where a downlink tuning action is available, it requests confirmation for a single tune. This view has no calculated Doppler offset in Hz and does not continuously retune your radio. Follow the satellite operator's procedure and use your radio or separately configured tracking software for correction.

The three views

Tracking emphasizes ground tracks and the map. Sky arranges selected satellites from the observer's perspective, useful for direction and elevation. Imagery shows the supported Earth-imagery content; an image timestamp is separate from the time used to calculate a satellite pass. Resize dividers if the catalog or detail pane is too small.

Use the Alerts controls to review notifications for favorites and relevant pass events. Check notification permission and the selected satellites when an expected alert is absent. Confirm actual satellite status, operating frequencies and the required radio setup from the operator's current information before transmission. The tracking display does not automatically aim an antenna, configure every radio or guarantee a successful satellite QSO.
